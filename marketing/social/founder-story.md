# Founder Story — Rain, PeekPick

Three lengths of the same true story. No hustle-porn, no "I quit my job to chase a dream" — because Rain didn't quit anything, they just noticed a problem and built the fix.

---

## 1. Facebook post length

I'm not a developer. No computer science background, never worked at a tech company. I'm a solo, non-technical founder here in Iloilo City, and a few months ago I built an app anyway.

Here's why. Scroll through any Iloilo buy-and-sell or community group on Facebook and you'll see the same pattern: someone posts a thing they don't need anymore, someone comments "pwede ba i-swap?", and then... nothing. No easy way to actually match what you have with what somebody else wants. We already have ukay-ukay culture. We already have bayanihan. We're already people who'd rather pass something along than throw it out. We just never had a tool actually built for that instinct.

So I made PeekPick. It works like a swipe app, but for things you own instead of people. You list what you have, you swipe on what other people have, and when you both like each other's stuff, it opens a chat so you can arrange the swap yourselves. Money's optional. Barter is the whole point.

I'm not a coder by trade — I learned enough, leaned on the right tools, and got a working app live. It has zero users right now. That's not a humble-brag, that's just where it's at. I'm looking for the first people in Iloilo willing to try it and tell me what's broken.

If that's you, comment below or message me. I'll walk you through it myself.

## 3. One-paragraph bio

Rain is a non-technical, solo founder based in Iloilo City who built PeekPick, a Tinder-style barter app that lets people swap the things they own instead of buying new. The idea came from something Rain kept seeing around the city — ukay-ukay culture, bayanihan, and Facebook groups full of "pwede ba i-swap?" comments that never went anywhere. PeekPick turns that instinct into an actual tool: list an item, swipe on what you want, match, chat, and arrange the trade yourselves. The app just launched with no users yet, and Rain is looking for Iloilo's first wave of swappers to help build it from here.
