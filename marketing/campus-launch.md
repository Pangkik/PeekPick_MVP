# Campus Launch — Recruiting the First Cohort Through Student Orgs

One solo founder can't run a launch event. The move is to find one student org that already runs events and give them a reason to run this one for you.

---

## Who to approach
Start with orgs that already touch move-in, org fairs, or community service:
- Student council / supreme student government (org fair access, mass reach)
- Environmental/sustainability orgs (Circular Passport is a direct fit for their mission)
- Dorm/residence hall councils (move-in swap is literally their calendar event)
- Org that runs community service hours or NSTP-adjacent programs (Circular Passport doubles as documentation)

Approach one org first, not five at once — you want a real partnership, not five lukewarm ones you can't service as a solo founder.

## Proposed low-effort partnership: the swap event

Structure that needs almost nothing from the org besides announcing it:

1. **Format:** "Dorm Move-In Swap" (start of semester) or "Pre-Break Swap" (end of semester, before students go home and declutter). Org just posts about it on their page/group chat and mentions it at one meeting — no venue, no logistics needed since matching happens in-app.
2. **Timeline:** 1-week window. Members download the app, list 2-3 items in week one. Org sends one reminder mid-week. Matches happen in-app; students arrange their own meet-up on campus (same day, same building — easiest possible logistics since everyone's already there).
3. **Rain's role:** answer DMs from confused first-time users, personally welcome the first handful who sign up, maybe do a 10-minute walkthrough at one org meeting if they want it (optional, not required).
4. **Org's role:** one announcement post, one reminder. That's it. Low-effort is the pitch — they get an event, students get a swap, org officer doesn't have to plan anything from scratch.
5. **No prizes needed to start.** If the org wants to add a small incentive later (e.g., most items traded gets a small recognition), that's their call and their budget — don't offer to fund anything as PeekPick, since there's no budget for it. ## The Circular Passport angle for schools

Some orgs and departments have community-service or sustainability requirements (NSTP, org-level environmental initiatives, scholarship community-service hours). Position the Circular Passport as something a student can point to:

> "Every trade you complete on PeekPick adds to your Circular Passport — items reused, kilos of waste diverted, CO2 saved, badges. It's not just a swap, it's a running record you can screenshot or show if your org or scholarship asks for proof of community/environmental involvement."

Be precise about what this actually is right now: a personal in-app record, not an official or third-party-certified document. If an org wants to accept it as formal proof for a requirement, that's the org's call to make — don't tell students it's automatically accepted anywhere. Frame it as "something worth showing," not "official documentation," unless a specific school confirms otherwise.
